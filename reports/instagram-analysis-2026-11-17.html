What the data says

Five findings

The winners all ask “is my phone or the internet doing something to me?”

Each one starts from a suspicion the viewer already has about the device in their pocket. The stalled Reels were about a developer tool, an automation course, a finance history story and a how-to checklist. Those audiences hardly exist yet on a 21-follower account.

The hook decided the result as much as the topic

Cookies is a privacy topic like phone listening, yet it stalled at 139 views with a 67% skip rate. Listening opens on a moment the viewer has lived. Cookies opens on a claim about faceless “companies”, then moves into HTTP and 1994 by second 10. The two openings are compared below.

Repeating a winning topic worked

Phone slowdown reached 1,451 views, and a second version a week later reached 1,402. The second version opened on a concrete fact (Apple’s €25M fine in France) and kept people watching 65% longer: 16.1 s against 9.8 s.

Reach does not turn into followers

Only 1.4% of views came from followers, so the algorithm keeps testing you on strangers, which is good. But after about 5,300 reach the account still has 21 followers. Shares are close to zero (22 in total), and saves are low except on CAPTCHA (15 of the 35).

The profile does not match the videos

The bio is in English (“Tech explained in 60 seconds”), but every video is in Darija. The handle is runtime.elhadi, while the videos say Runtime.tech. A visitor who arrives from a Reel sees a different account from the one they just watched.

Recommended approach

What to change

  1. Focus the channel on everyday phone and internet mysteries, in Darija, for ordinary people. Pause the n8n 16-episode series and the Claude Code content on this account. A long series works once people already follow you for it, so bring them back later or try them as carousels.
  2. Use one hook formula: a lived moment, then the denial, then a promise. In the first 2 seconds, show the phone with something suspicious on it (a glowing mic dot, a falling battery, a notification). Keep history and definitions after second 40.
  3. Make it a named series with a verdict, for example صح ولا كذب؟, ending every Reel on a large TRUE / FALSE / HALF-TRUE stamp. The stamp is already part of your style system. A series gives people a reason to follow.
  4. End on something worth saving or sending. A 3-point checklist on the last frame (“check these 3 settings”) drives saves, which is probably why CAPTCHA got 15. A direct line such as “send it to the friend who always says…” drives shares.
  5. Fix the profile so visitors follow. Rewrite the bio in Darija, make the handle match Runtime.tech, and pin the three strongest Reels: slowdown v2, listening and CAPTCHA.

About this data

Source: Instagram insights through Windsor.ai, read on 17 Nov 2026. The trial covers only the last 30 days. Likes, comments and audience demographics were not available: demographics need 100 followers, and the current Instagram connection does not return likes or comments. Daily reach and views are summed across days, so the same person can be counted more than once.

Ten Reels over one month is a small sample. The topic and skip-rate pattern is strong, but it is not enough to choose the best posting day or time.
